01 — The mark

Two rings,
one promise.

Vow is two O’s interlocked — the oldest sign of a bond, and a chain drawn in one gesture. Each ring passes over and under the other, so neither can be pulled free. That is the promise of a self-custody wallet: you and your keys, bound together.

02 — Lockups

Mark and name.

The wordmark is Geist Semibold, tracked tight at −4%. Use the horizontal lockup by default; stacked for square spaces; the mark alone once the name is already on screen.

03 — Solid & misuse

When colour can’t travel.

For print, embossing, favicons and partner lockups, use the solid mark in Ink or white. Never rebuild the glow by hand — use the supplied files.

✕ Don’t rotate
✕ Don’t stretch
✕ Don’t recolour the glow
✕ Don’t use the light mark on dark

06 — Voice

Say it like a promise.

Short, certain, plain. We state what’s true and what we won’t do. No hype, no moon talk, no jargon a first-time owner wouldn’t know.

✓ Say“Your keys stay on this phone.”✕ “Military-grade decentralised security.”
✓ Say“Sent. It’ll arrive in about a minute.”✕ “Transaction broadcast to mempool!”
✓ Say“We can’t recover this for you.”✕ “Oops! Something went wrong 😅”

Boilerplate

Oath is a self-custody crypto wallet, available now on the App Store for iPhone, with Android coming soon. Keys are generated and stored on the user’s device — Oath never holds them and cannot move funds. The app lets people hold, send, receive and swap digital assets with plain-language security built in.
